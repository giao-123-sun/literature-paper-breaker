# Research Proposal

## Title
The Algorithmic Flaneur: Psychogeography, Attention Economics, and the Phenomenology of AI-Mediated Urban Experience

## Thesis

This essay argues that AI-driven navigation, recommendation, and location-based services constitute a fundamental transformation of the phenomenology of urban experience -- one that can be understood as the emergence of an "algorithmic flaneur" whose relationship to the city is mediated, curated, and constrained by computational systems in ways that are structurally analogous to, yet ontologically distinct from, the forms of urban mediation analyzed by Benjamin, Debord, and de Certeau. Drawing on postphenomenology (Ihde, Verbeek), attention economics (Williams, Crawford), and critical urban theory (Mattern, Zuboff), the essay develops the concept of "algorithmic psychogeography" to describe how recommendation algorithms, navigation apps, and platform interfaces function as a new layer of urban architecture -- not built from material substrates but from data flows, attention captures, and behavioral nudges that reshape the phenomenological structure of spatial encounter, serendipity, and dwelling.

The central claim is threefold: (1) that algorithmic mediation of urban experience represents not merely a new tool for navigating cities but a new *mode of revealing* (in Heidegger's sense) that transforms space itself into standing-reserve for data extraction; (2) that the attention economy operates as a form of *spatial architecture* that restructures the perceptual field of the urban subject, collapsing the distinction between physical and informational environments; and (3) that recovering and radicalizing the psychogeographic tradition -- particularly the derive as a practice of cultivating serendipitous encounter -- offers resources for an ethics of algorithmic wandering that resists the total determination of spatial experience by computational systems.

## Methodology

The essay employs a hybrid methodology combining:

1. **Phenomenological Analysis**: Following Ihde and Verbeek's postphenomenological approach, the essay analyzes the human-technology relations constituted by navigation apps, recommendation algorithms, and location-based services. This involves describing the specific forms of technological mediation at work -- how these technologies transform perception, attention, and spatial orientation -- using Ihde's taxonomy of embodiment, hermeneutic, alterity, and background relations.

2. **Critical-Genealogical Method**: Following Benjamin and Debord, the essay traces a genealogy of mediated urban walking from the 19th-century flaneur through the Situationist derive to contemporary algorithmic navigation. This genealogical approach reveals both continuities and ruptures in how technology mediates the urban subject's relationship to space.

3. **Conceptual Analysis and Philosophical Argumentation**: The essay develops original philosophical concepts -- "algorithmic psychogeography," "attentional architecture," "the collapse of serendipity" -- through close engagement with primary texts in phenomenology, critical theory, and urban studies. Arguments are developed through dialectical engagement with existing positions.

4. **Critical Theory of Technology**: Drawing on Heidegger's critique of enframing, Zuboff's analysis of surveillance capitalism, and Mattern's critique of the "city-as-computer" metaphor, the essay situates algorithmic urban mediation within broader structures of technological domination and capitalist accumulation.

## Expected Contributions

1. **Conceptual Innovation**: The concept of the "algorithmic flaneur" and "algorithmic psychogeography" provide new theoretical vocabulary for understanding the lived experience of platform-mediated urbanism, bridging psychogeographic theory with philosophy of technology.

2. **Interdisciplinary Synthesis**: The essay brings together traditions that rarely communicate directly -- Situationist psychogeography, postphenomenology, attention economics, and platform urbanism studies -- generating insights unavailable from within any single tradition.

3. **Ethical Framework**: The proposed "ethics of algorithmic wandering" contributes a new dimension to the ethics of AI by addressing the phenomenological and existential stakes of algorithmic mediation, beyond standard concerns with bias and transparency.

4. **Critical Diagnostics**: By analyzing specific technologies (Google Maps, Yelp, Instagram location features) through philosophical lenses, the essay demonstrates the value of philosophical critique for understanding concrete socio-technical phenomena.
