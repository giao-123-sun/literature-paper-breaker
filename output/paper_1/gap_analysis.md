# Gap Analysis: LLMs as Cultural Agents and Collective Memory

## Overview
Based on the systematic review of 20 published works across digital memory studies, AI and public discourse, algorithmic mediation, and cultural bias in LLMs, this analysis identifies five genuine research gaps that the proposed study aims to address.

---

## Gap 1: Absence of an Integrated Theoretical Framework Connecting Collective Memory Theory with LLM Studies

**Evidence from the literature**: Collective memory scholars (Gensburger & Clavert 2024; Smit 2024; Hoskins 2024) have begun asking whether AI reshapes memory practices, while AI bias researchers (Tao et al. 2025; Tao et al. 2024; Navigli et al. 2023) document Western-centric hegemony in LLM outputs. However, these two literatures operate largely in parallel. Memory studies scholars invoke Halbwachs and his successors but rarely engage with the technical architecture of LLMs. Conversely, AI fairness researchers document cultural biases without connecting their findings to the theoretical apparatus of collective memory.

**The gap**: No existing study provides an integrated theoretical framework that systematically connects Halbwachs's collective memory theory—particularly his concepts of social frameworks of memory and mnemonic socialization—with the specific mechanisms through which LLMs process, encode, and reproduce historical narratives. Such a framework would allow scholars to understand not just *that* LLMs reshape memory, but *how* they do so through identifiable sociotechnical processes.

---

## Gap 2: Limited Empirical Investigation of LLM Outputs as Memory Texts

**Evidence from the literature**: While Pesta et al. (2024) propose using Historical LLMs to simulate past mentalities, and Tao et al. (2025) develop frameworks for evaluating cultural bias in LLM outputs, no study has systematically analyzed LLM-generated text *as a form of memory text*—that is, as a cultural artifact that actively shapes how users understand historical events. Studies of platformization and memory (Keightley & Schlesinger 2024; Makhortykh 2023) focus on social media platforms rather than conversational AI. Yasseri (2025) theorizes about LLMs and collective memory but does not present empirical analysis of actual LLM outputs.

**The gap**: There is no published empirical study that treats LLM-generated historical narratives as "memory texts" subject to discourse analysis—examining their narrative structures, framing devices, inclusions, exclusions, and epistemic authority claims. This leaves a significant empirical lacuna in understanding how LLMs function as cultural agents of memory.

---

## Gap 3: Neglect of the Interaction Between User Queries and LLM Memory Production

**Evidence from the literature**: Hoskins (2024) notes that ChatGPT's memory features represent a "personalization of surveillance" and Gensburger & Clavert (2024) observe that AI-formed memories "evolve based on user interactions." However, existing research treats users primarily as passive recipients of algorithmically curated content (Makhortykh 2023; Adriaansen & Smit 2025). The interactive, conversational nature of LLM engagement—where users co-produce historical narratives through their prompts, follow-up questions, and framing choices—has not been empirically investigated.

**The gap**: The dialogic process through which users and LLMs co-construct historical understanding remains unexamined. Unlike social media platforms where algorithms curate pre-existing content, LLMs generate novel text in response to user prompts, creating a fundamentally different mode of memory mediation that existing frameworks have not theorized or studied empirically.

---

## Gap 4: Insufficient Attention to LLMs' Role in Homogenizing Contested Histories

**Evidence from the literature**: Multiple studies document Western-centric bias in LLMs (Tao et al. 2024; Navigli et al. 2023) and the risk of narrative homogenization (Yasseri 2025; Hutson & Plate 2025). Research on AI and misinformation (Bruns et al. 2025) shows LLMs can generate convincing but misleading content. However, no study has specifically investigated how LLMs handle *contested historical events*—events where multiple legitimate interpretations exist, such as colonial histories, civil conflicts, or indigenous displacement—and whether they tend toward homogenized, consensus-driven accounts that flatten legitimate historiographical debate.

**The gap**: The specific mechanisms through which LLMs navigate, simplify, or flatten contested histories—and the consequences for pluralistic collective memory—remain unstudied. This is particularly urgent given that LLMs are increasingly used as informational resources by students, journalists, and the general public.

---

## Gap 5: Lack of Comparative Analysis Across LLM Platforms and Languages

**Evidence from the literature**: Tao et al. (2024) compare cultural bias across five LLMs for 107 countries but focus on value alignment rather than historical narrative production. Most studies examine a single model (typically ChatGPT) in English. Hutson & Plate (2025) note that training dataset size does not guarantee diversity, and Adriaansen & Smit (2025) identify Western-centric bias as a systemic issue, but no study systematically compares how different LLM platforms (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, etc.) and different language prompts produce divergent historical narratives about the same events.

**The gap**: Cross-platform and cross-linguistic comparative analysis of LLM-generated historical narratives is absent from the literature, despite evidence that different training corpora, alignment procedures, and linguistic contexts may produce substantially different memory texts. Such comparison would reveal the degree to which memory homogenization is a feature of specific platforms versus a structural property of the LLM paradigm.

---

## Summary of Gaps

| Gap | Domain | Significance |
|-----|--------|-------------|
| 1. No integrated theory connecting Halbwachs with LLM mechanisms | Theoretical | Foundational for the field |
| 2. LLM outputs not analyzed as memory texts | Empirical/Methodological | Opens new analytical approach |
| 3. User-LLM interaction in memory co-production unexplored | Empirical | Captures novel mode of mediation |
| 4. LLM handling of contested histories unstudied | Empirical/Political | High social urgency |
| 5. No cross-platform/cross-linguistic comparison | Comparative/Empirical | Reveals structural dynamics |

These gaps collectively point toward a research program that integrates memory theory with empirical analysis of LLM outputs, treating LLMs not merely as information retrieval tools but as cultural agents that actively participate in the construction and transmission of collective memory.
