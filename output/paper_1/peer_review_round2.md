### Strengths

1. **Political economy integration**: The revised Section 2.3 directly addresses my central critique. The analysis of how corporate decisions about training data, RLHF evaluation, and content policies constitute the AMF mechanisms — rather than merely producing them as side effects — represents a significant theoretical advance.

2. **Governance recommendations**: The three-pronged governance framework (epistemic transparency, pluralistic alignment, community-based oversight) is concrete, actionable, and well-grounded in the empirical findings. The Memory Community Advisory Boards proposal is particularly compelling.

3. **Data labor acknowledgment**: The mention of Perrigo (2023) and the labor conditions of Global South annotators is welcome, though I would still encourage further development of this dimension.

### Weaknesses

1. **Commercial logic could be pushed further**: While the paper now acknowledges the commercial imperatives driving LLM design, it could push harder on the specific ways in which the engagement-driven business model of AI companies systematically incentivizes the production of authoritative-seeming, satisfying responses over epistemically honest, pluralistic ones.

2. **Platform comparison of corporate structures**: The paper compares outputs across platforms but does not compare the corporate structures, business models, and alignment philosophies of the three companies — differences that may explain some of the cross-platform variations documented.

### Decision: **Minor Revision**

**Overall Score**: 8.0 / 10

### Strengths

1. **Reframing from "bias" to structural inequality**: The paper now uses the language of structural epistemic inequality and epistemic violence where appropriate, while maintaining analytical precision about different types of representational harm. This is exactly the kind of reframing I advocated.

2. **Intersectional analysis**: The analysis of how LLM narratives marginalize multiply marginalized groups — particularly the discussion of gendered colonial violence and Lugones's "coloniality of gender" — adds an important dimension previously absent.

3. **Postcolonial theoretical engagement**: The engagement with Spivak, Mignolo, and Rothberg is substantive and productive, not merely citational.

4. **Community-based governance**: The Memory Community Advisory Boards proposal centers the voices of affected communities in AI governance — an approach consistent with the principles of community-based research.

### Weaknesses

1. **Participatory methods still lacking**: While the paper now acknowledges the limitation of its user study sample and integrates an intersectional lens, the fundamental design still positions marginalized communities as objects of study. Future iterations should employ participatory action research methods.

2. **Limited engagement with Black digital studies**: The paper could engage more deeply with the growing field of Black digital studies and the specific ways in which anti-Black racism is encoded in algorithmic systems.

### Decision: **Minor Revision**

| Methodology | 7 | The measure validation details and event selection rationale are improved. The intersectional lens is welcome. |
| Literature Engagement | 9 | The paper now engages comprehensively with the memory studies canon. The integration of Erll, Rothberg, A. Assmann, Koselleck, and Levy/Sznaider demonstrates genuine disciplinary expertise. |
| Argumentation & Evidence | 8 | The argument is strengthened by the deeper theoretical engagement. The concept of "mnemonic compartmentalization" is analytically useful. |
| Clarity & Writing | 9 | Continues to be exceptionally well written. The theoretical additions are integrated without sacrificing clarity. |
| Significance | 9 | This is now positioned to be a landmark contribution to the emerging field of AI and collective memory studies, with lasting significance for both memory studies and critical AI scholarship. |

**Overall Score**: 8.6 / 10

### Strengths

1. **Algorithmic memory travel**: The concept of "algorithmic memory travel" as a distinct mode of transcultural memory transmission — governed by statistical patterns rather than human meaning-making — is a genuinely original theoretical contribution that advances the travelling memory framework into new terrain.

2. **Temporal sophistication**: The engagement with Koselleck's "space of experience" and "horizon of expectation" and the nuanced claim that the issue is not whether LLMs are presentist but how their presentism differs from other forms demonstrates sophisticated engagement with the temporality of memory.

3. **Cultural agent theorization**: The revised theorization of LLM agency as "distributed agency" (Latour) rather than intentional agency addresses my concern about conceptual precision while maintaining the paper's central claim about LLMs' cultural-mnemonic efficacy.

4. **Multidirectional memory**: The application of Rothberg's framework to reveal "mnemonic compartmentalization" — LLMs' tendency to treat different histories as separate narratives rather than recognizing their interconnections — is an important finding.

### Weaknesses

1. **Aleida Assmann's canon/archive distinction**: I noted in my first review that A. Assmann's distinction between "canon" and "archive" in cultural memory maps productively onto the paper's analysis. This engagement is still implicit rather than explicit. A brief discussion would strengthen the theoretical framework.

2. **Non-human agency**: While the Latourian framing of distributed agency is appropriate, the paper could benefit from brief engagement with posthumanist memory theory to strengthen its claims about non-human participation in memory cultures.

### Decision: **Accept with Minor Revisions**

## Editor Synthesis

**Editor**: Associate Editor, *Memory Studies*

### Meta-Review

The three reviewers agree that the revised manuscript represents a substantial improvement over the original submission. The central critiques raised in Round 1 — the absence of political economy analysis (Reviewer 1), insufficient engagement with critical race and postcolonial theory (Reviewer 2), and underengagement with the memory studies canon (Reviewer 3) — have been addressed comprehensively and effectively. All three reviewers now recommend publication after minor revisions, with Reviewer 3 recommending acceptance with minor revisions.

### Key Improvements Noted

1. The integration of political economy analysis transforms the AMF from a descriptive into an explanatory framework
2. The reframing from "bias" to "structural epistemic inequality" more accurately captures the phenomena documented
3. The engagement with transcultural memory, travelling memory, and multidirectional memory deepens the paper's contribution to memory studies
4. The governance recommendations provide concrete, actionable proposals
5. The concept of "algorithmic memory travel" represents a genuine theoretical innovation

### Remaining Issues

1. Brief additional engagement with Aleida Assmann's canon/archive distinction (Reviewer 3)
2. Minor expansion of the commercial logic analysis in the business model discussion (Reviewer 1)
3. Note on future methodological directions toward participatory approaches (Reviewer 2)

### Decision

**Accept with Minor Revisions**

The editors are pleased with the thoroughness of the revision and confident that the remaining issues can be addressed in a final round of minor edits. This paper is positioned to make a significant and lasting contribution to the intersection of memory studies and critical AI scholarship.
